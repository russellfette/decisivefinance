## Situation

A Series B SaaS company in the developer-tools category was 16 months past close and five months into a Series C process that had gone quiet. The board deck was showing net revenue retention at 108% and gross margin at 78%. Both numbers were inside the band the lead investor wanted to see. Neither number was tracking what either party thought it was tracking.

The CEO had stopped forwarding investor updates to the leadership team around month twelve. The lead investor had started citing narrative instead of numbers in the firm's partner meetings. Four of the six walking dead signals were live. The board had asked its first orientation question in the last meeting: *what did we learn this quarter?* The CEO answered without flinching; the question was not flagged in the minutes.

The independent director opened the conversation that brought us in. Framed it as a two-week external scan with a cash recovery guarantee. The CEO accepted inside 48 hours.

## Diagnosis

### Trap stack

Three of the six cognitive traps were live, with a fourth in formation.

1. **Cross-subsidy blindness.** The SDR motion was attributed to inbound pipeline in the deck. In the underlying data it was producing 4% of closed-won ARR at a loaded cost of 18% of GTM spend. The ratio was not computed in the standard reporting.
2. **Default flip.** The SDR team had been built at Series A+ to feed the enterprise motion that never matured. No one had installed a re-decide trigger. The motion was running on eighteen-month-old assumptions.
3. **First-principles decay.** The LTV/CAC ratio on the board deck (4.2x) was computed on a customer definition that had changed after the 2024 pricing consolidation. On the current ICP, the same ratio was 2.6x.
4. **Forming: marginal analysis framing.** The board was being asked to treat SDR headcount as sunk cost.

### Ratio restatement

Before any Three-Path work, we restated three ratios on the current customer definition:

- Net revenue retention: 108% on the old cohort definition, 94% on the current ICP cohort.
- Gross margin: 78% on the deck, 71% after removing a one-time credit from Q3.
- LTV/CAC: 4.2x on the deck, 2.6x on the current-ICP cohort.

The ratio mirage was specific, measurable, and traceable to two definition changes the reporting had not been updated to reflect.

### Days 15 to 22: the first Three-Path Model

The first Three-Path Model was on the SDR motion, the decision that most changed the twelve-month burn.

- **Keep:** $3.8M of GTM spend next twelve months, projected $2.1M of ARR contribution. Pre-mortem: three scenarios in which Keep fails, all driven by the enterprise motion failing to mature faster than it had in the prior eighteen months.
- **Kill:** $3.8M of burn recovered over twelve months. Six FTE redeployed to customer success, projected to lift NRR by 3 to 5 points on the current cohort. One-time severance cost: $280K.
- **Restructure:** reduce SDR headcount from 11 to 4, reassign the four to the one vertical showing real enterprise traction. Projected twelve-month spend: $1.4M. Contribution margin projected positive by quarter three.

The CEO signed Kill. The board saw all three paths at the next meeting. The conversation was the first operating-posture board meeting in six quarters. The GP lead's partner-meeting summary the following week referenced numbers again, not narrative.
