## At a glance

- Almost everyone in a sale is paid when it closes, which means their numbers are read, by the buyer, as advocacy. That discount is baked in before anyone speaks.
- The one voice a buyer's analyst can trust is the one with nothing riding on the transaction. Independence from the deal is not a compliance footnote, it's what makes the numbers believable.
- We are the one advisor in the deal who is not paid on the deal, and our fees are tied to the value created, never to the deal.

There's a structural reason a buyer distrusts a seller's numbers before he's even read them: he knows who's paid to get the deal done. This piece is about why independence from the transaction is what makes numbers believable, and why it's the product rather than a disclaimer.

## Why does a buyer discount the seller's advisors?

Because incentives are visible, and a buyer's analyst reads them first. The banker is paid a percentage when it closes. The broker is paid when it closes. Even well-meaning advisors are oriented toward getting to yes, because yes is when they're paid. So when their materials present the earnings, the buyer applies a silent discount: this is the number from the people who profit if I believe it.

That discount is rational, and it's expensive for the seller. The more the surrounding advice is tied to the close, the less any single number from that side is worth, because the buyer can't separate the fact from the sales.

## What makes a number believable, then?

A source with nothing riding on the transaction. When the person who prepared and stands behind the numbers is paid the same whether the deal closes, stalls, or dies, the buyer loses his reason to read the numbers as advocacy. The independence is the credibility, and the credibility is what protects the price.

This is the position we hold on purpose:

1. **We don't sell the company.** We don't solicit buyers, negotiate terms, or advise on the form of the deal.
2. **We're not paid on the close.** Our fees are tied to the value created, never to the deal, so nothing we say is bent toward getting to yes.
3. **We work the numbers, not the transaction.** Our only job is to make the earnings true and believable, which serves the seller and survives the buyer's read at the same time.

Because we have nothing riding on the close, the number we put on the page is one both the owner and the buyer's analyst can trust, and that trust is worth real dollars at the table.

## Why this protects the deal for everyone

For the owner, independence turns his numbers from advocacy into evidence, which is what holds the multiple through diligence. For the advisor running the process, bringing in an independent party to make the numbers believable removes the single most common cause of a re-trade without adding another voice angling for a piece of the close. Believable numbers protect the multiple from diligence discounts, and believability requires a source the buyer has no reason to doubt.
