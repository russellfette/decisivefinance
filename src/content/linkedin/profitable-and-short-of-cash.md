Some of the tightest cash months I see land in a company's best year.

Here's how it goes. Sales are up, the team's busy, the P&L says it's working. Then payroll week arrives and the owner's moving money between accounts to make it.

The profit's real. It's sitting in receivables and inventory, and the books were set up to report it, so nobody's telling the owner when it turns back into cash.

Here's an illustration, not a client figure. An $18M company whose customers drift from paying in 45 days to 58 days has about $640,000 less in the bank. Same sales, same margin. Thirteen days nobody decided on.

The fix is plain. A 13-week cash forecast: receipts and payments by week, rolled forward every Monday, checked against what actually happened. After a month or two the surprises stop, and so do the late nights moving money around.
