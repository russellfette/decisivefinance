A buyer can agree that every dollar of your revenue is real and still take $270,000 out of your earnings.

Diligence does not test whether a sale happened. It tests when it was earned. That is cutoff testing.

The arithmetic: move $900,000 of revenue one quarter later and it takes its margin with it. At a 30% contribution rate, $270,000 comes off normalized EBITDA. Earnings is the number the multiple multiplies, so the cost is a multiple of $270,000.

Nothing was faked. Some of it was early. Shipments booked at the dock instead of on transfer of control. Progress billings taken on the invoice date. Annual contracts booked up front. Each is arguable alone. Together they establish a direction, and direction is what a buyer prices.

The larger cost never shows up on a schedule. Once a buyer believes the calendar in your financials is negotiable, he stops taking any period at face value.

What owners find hard to believe: this one is entirely inside your control. Cutoff is a policy question, not a performance question. Write the policy down, apply it to every month in the trailing twelve, and re-cut the periods.

The constraint is timing. Fix it the same quarter you go to market and you have documented that the problem was recent, not removed it. The owners who clear cutoff testing without a scratch fixed it a year before a buyer was in the room.

If you are inside two years of a sale, this is worth an hour of your attention before it is worth a buyer's. Full piece linked in the comments.
