Most 2027 plans I see add people at the same slope as revenue. Grow 15%, grow the team 15%.

Payroll doesn't move that way. It moves in steps. One team can carry a lot more with the people it has. Another is already at the wall, and nobody planned for it.

Here's an illustration, not a client figure. A $22M distributor plans $25M and budgets five seats at $95,000 each. Check capacity team by team: sales can carry the growth, customer service has room once one account's requests are routed properly, the warehouse needs one person by spring, purchasing needs one by fall. Two hires instead of five. That's $285,000 of payroll you don't carry, on the same plan.

It cuts the other way too. Wait on the warehouse seat and you pay in overtime and tired people in your busiest months.

The fix is a trigger, not a date: we add the seat when the team crosses a volume it can't hold. And a hire made too early is hard on the person you hired, too.

Which seat in your plan has a trigger behind it, and which one has only a month?
