An owner told me his company earns $2.18M. He was off by $310,000, in his own favor, and he couldn't prove it.

He wasn't guessing. He was reading his tax return, the one earnings number most owners quote with confidence.

Here's the problem with that.

The return answers what's taxable this year. A buyer asks what the company earns, on accrual, in a normal year, without you in it. Those pull opposite directions. A good return defers income and accelerates deductions, because that's what it's for. A buyer's model reverses every one of those calls.

On his $20M company, two adjustments did most of it. $205,000 of December work billed and collected in January. $105,000 of prepaids deducted when the check cleared instead of spread over the period covered.

Same twelve months, same business. $2.49M instead of $2.18M. At 6x, that gap is $1,860,000.

Nobody took it from him. He'd been quoting the low number to his own banker, carrying it for years as what his life's work was worth.

Then there's the calendar. A 2026 return goes out on the September 2027 extension, nine months late. Get a PE call in March and your only finished document is about 2025.

The fix isn't clever. Close the year on accrual by the end of January whether or not anything's happening, and build the tax-to-accrual bridge once.

I do that in 14 days, with one guarantee: at least 3x the fee in owner-accepted value, or you pay nothing.

If your most recent finished number is a return, is that what you'd want a buyer starting from?
