The buyer's Quality of Earnings is built by people paid to find reasons to pay you less. You can run the same exam first.

Same test, opposite side of the table. When the buyer commissions it, every gray area resolves in his favor. When you commission it early, every gray area turns into something you get to fix.

Sell-side QoE prep at a regional firm typically runs $25,000 to $75,000. On a company priced at a multiple of earnings, the price it protects is a large multiple of that.

Here's what the seller controls, which is almost everything a QoE tests:

1. The add-back schedule, built so you can defend every line.
2. One revenue recognition rule, applied every month.
3. Margin by product and customer, full cost loaded, so there's no loser you haven't already found.
4. Working capital that reconciles to purpose.
5. A data room where every claim has a file behind it.

The catch is time. A QoE reads the trailing period. Fix a mispriced line the quarter you go to market and the buyer sees a recent change. Fix it a year ahead and it's just how the business runs.

That's what I keep coming back to. A finding the buyer makes becomes a discount. The same finding, made by you first, becomes a fix.

If you're within two years of a sale, or you advise owners who are, is that head start on the calendar yet? Full piece linked in the comments.
