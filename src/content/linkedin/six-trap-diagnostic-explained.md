Capital does not get trapped by accident. It gets trapped by decisions, and the decisions are defended.

Six cognitive traps do the defending. I score all six in every Diagnostic, with evidence:

1. Sunk Cost. The money already spent gets a vote it does not deserve. The $2.1M of engineering in the line nobody will kill.

2. Status Quo. Doing nothing feels neutral, so allocations roll forward from a context that no longer exists.

3. Mental Accounting. The right total hides the wrong mix. The budget is on plan, so nobody asks about the allocation inside it.

4. Anchoring. Two market-years after the raise, the plan still triangulates back to the valuation story that justified the round.

5. Loss Aversion. The pain of naming a loss runs roughly twice the value of recovering the capital. The trap is not the bad position. It is the unstarted conversation.

6. Certainty Illusion. Clean dashboards and a twelve-tab model dress a dead plan in confidence. The polish protects the other five traps from review.

Scoring is LIVE, LATENT, or NOT PRESENT, and every call needs evidence. "It feels like sunk cost" is not a finding. Four FTEs on a line below 25% of projected revenue for 14 months, plus the founder saying "we've already invested too much to walk away," is a finding.

Where two or more traps are live around the same decision, the trapped value is there. Every time.
