Revenue's up. Profit isn't. And your numbers can't tell you why.

It's the most common thing I hear from owners of growing companies, and it's almost never one problem. It's three or four of them hiding inside one blended margin.

A line you're growing that was mispriced from the start. A cost that drifted up while your prices sat still. A mix tilting toward your lowest-margin work. One big customer on terms nobody has looked at since you signed them.

None of that shows up as a labeled line on a P&L. It shows up as a flat bottom line and a shrug.

Here's the uncomfortable part. A buyer sees it before you do. Splitting margin apart by product, job, and customer is what his analyst does all day. He finds the low-margin growth, calls it deteriorating profitability, and prices it into his offer.

Which is also the opportunity, if you get there first.

I worked with a $15M manufacturer whose largest customer turned out to be his least profitable one. Once margin was broken out and loaded with full cost, margins moved from 15% to 22% in 90 days. Same customers, same plant, different decisions.

So the margin isn't lost. It's trapped, and it's trapped inside a report that was built to close the month, not to decide anything.

Every month you wait, it compounds, and you're a month closer to a buyer quantifying it for you. Full breakdown in the comments.

If revenue is up and profit is flat at your place, do you know which line is doing it?
