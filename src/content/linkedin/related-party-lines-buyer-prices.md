You rent the building to your own company. That lease is worth about $600,000 of your sale price, and right now it's pointing the wrong way.

Say the company pays $60,000 a year on a building that would rent for $180,000 on the open market. Your P&L looks $120,000 better than the business performs. A buyer normalizes the rent back to market, earnings drop $120,000, and at a 5x multiple that's $600,000 off the price.

Nothing improper happened. You set the rent when you owned both sides and had no reason to set it anywhere else.

What gets expensive is the asymmetry. Corrections in the buyer's favor need no support. Corrections in your favor need documents. So the brother-in-law at $95,000 and the $40,000 of truck, travel and meals, both legitimate add-backs, only come back to you with a paper trail. That spread runs about $255,000 of earnings, or $1,275,000 at 5x.

Three documents settle each line. The written agreement. A market benchmark showing the rate is arm's length. Evidence of what happens to the item at closing.

Most owners have two of the three. The missing one is the market benchmark, because nobody needed it while you sat on both sides. It's the cheapest one to get.

One more thing: fixing the lease today doesn't clean up the trailing twelve months a buyer reads. It starts a clock. This work belongs quarters ahead of a sale, not weeks.

If you're inside two years of selling, could you support your related-party lines on paper today?
