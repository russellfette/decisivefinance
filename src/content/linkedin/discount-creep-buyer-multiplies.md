Three points of quiet discounting on a $20M company is $600,000 of earnings. At a 5x multiple, that habit is $3,000,000 of sale price.

And almost no owner can name the number, because it never appears anywhere.

There is no account called Discounts We Did Not Mean to Give. There is only list price, realized price, and a gap that widens a little every year.

A concession to close a quarter. A legacy rate a good customer kept for eight years. A rep's standing 10% that became the real list.

Each one was defensible when it was made. Stacked up, they reprice the whole company.

Here is the test I run. Pull twelve months of invoices. Compare realized price to current list, by customer and by product. Total the gap in dollars. On companies between $5M and $50M, an average discount of 2% to 4% that nobody consciously approved is common. On $20M of revenue, each point is $200,000, and nearly all of it would have reached earnings.

Then sort every discount into two piles: earned or inherited. Earned buys volume or terms you can name. Inherited buys nothing. It is just an old decision still collecting a toll.

A buyer cares twice. The leak sits inside the earnings he multiplies. And your pricing discipline is one of the signals he reads to decide whether to believe your numbers at all.

I find this in 14 days, with one guarantee: at least 3x the fee in owner-accepted value, or you pay nothing.
