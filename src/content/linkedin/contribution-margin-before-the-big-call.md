The biggest customer you could land next year might be the one that makes you work harder and keep less.

I see it a lot with owners of growing companies. A contract shows up that doubles volume with one customer, at a better price for them. It feels like the year's win. Then the team's stretched, the bank balance hasn't moved, and nobody can say why.

The answer is usually one number: contribution margin. What a dollar of revenue leaves after the costs that move with it.

Here's an illustration, not a client figure. At a 30% contribution margin, a 5% price cut on a big contract takes a sixth of what that work contributes. You'd need a lot more volume just to stand still.

Most P&Ls can't show that number. They were set up to file the return and close the month, so the costs that move with revenue and the costs that don't sit mixed together. So the owner decides on feel, and carries the weight of it alone.
