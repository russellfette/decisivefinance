A month you already lived through can cost you $1,320,000 on the day it drops off your trailing twelve.

Here's what I mean. A buyer prices your last twelve months. That window is fixed at twelve and it moves, so every month is two events, not one. A month arrives, and a month leaves.

Everybody watches the month arriving. It's the one you're still working, it lands close to your run rate, and it feels unremarkable.

The month leaving is the same month a year ago, and it carries whatever happened back then. The stocking order that never repeated. The week of overtime. The settlement.

Take a manufacturer at $30M of revenue and $3.6M of trailing EBITDA, so $300,000 a month on average. Last September was a record, $520,000, on one large order from the number two customer. The day this September closes, that $520,000 comes out of the window and an ordinary $300,000 goes in. Trailing EBITDA drops $220,000. At 6x, that's $1,320,000 of enterprise value, gone on the last day of a month where nothing about the business changed.

Run it the other way and the same arithmetic pays you. A bad month rolling off is a tailwind you didn't have to earn.

Here's the part that gets me. That schedule is knowable a year out. It's sitting in your own general ledger. Almost nobody looks, so owners go to market in the worst window of the next twelve and never find out it was a choice.

Your trailing twelve isn't weather. It's arithmetic you can read.

Do you know which month yours drops next?
