Ask an owner what the company spends on software and you'll get a number. Ask him to name every subscription and who uses it, and the room goes quiet.

I see it a lot in growing companies. Nothing was wasted on purpose. A tool for a project. A consultant for ninety days. A vendor renewal with a new price. Each one made sense the day it started, and then it just kept renewing.

Here's an illustration, not a client figure. A $16M company whose overhead went from 18% of revenue to 21% over three years is spending $480,000 a year more than the same business needed. Nobody approved $480,000. They approved forty small things.

The fix isn't a memo telling every team to trim 10%. That lands on the people doing the profitable work. It's one list of every recurring cost, with an owner, what it was meant to buy, and the date it can change. Then one question per line: would we sign this today?

Most owners find two or three lines where the answer is no. And they stop being the only person in the building asking "do we still need this?"
