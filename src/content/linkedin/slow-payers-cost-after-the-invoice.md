Ask an owner who his slowest payer is and he'll answer before you finish the question. Ask what that customer costs him and the room goes quiet.

The margin report stops at the invoice. It treats a customer who pays in 20 days the same as one who pays in 80. The waiting lands somewhere else: interest on the line, the bookkeeper's last week of the month, a write off nobody saw coming.

Here's an illustration, not a client figure. A customer buys $1.6M a year on 30 day terms and pays in 80. That's about $219,000 the company earned and can't use. On a line at 8.5%, about $18,600 a year in interest, more than a point of margin on that account.

Nobody decided to let it drift. It went 45, then 60, then 80, and each month the check came, so each month it felt fine.

The fix is one number per account and one decision: hold the terms, change them, price it in, or leave it on purpose.

And somebody on your team stops sweating payroll week.
