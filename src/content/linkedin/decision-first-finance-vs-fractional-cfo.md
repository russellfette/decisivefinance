A fractional CFO fills a chair. The question is what the chair does.

The standard engagement stabilizes the finance function: close the books, build the board pack, manage the audit. Necessary work. I respect it. And it is retrospective by design: an increasingly polished account of what already happened.

Here is the test I give founders. Look at your last three months of finance output and count the forward decisions it produced. Not reports. Decisions: named calls, with dollars under each path and a date.

Most stalled portcos count zero.

Decision-first finance inverts the calendar:

1. Every material call gets a Three-Path Model. Keep, Kill, Restructure, dollars under each, a recommendation I will defend in front of your board.

2. Every committed dollar gets scored against current context, not the context it was approved in.

3. The close exists to feed the decision review, not the other way around.

When is the standard fractional CFO the right buy? When the books are the problem. Pre-Series A, unreliable close, no model: hire the hygiene first.

The gap I built for sits after that. Books mostly clean, $1M to $8M ARR, 18 months past the raise, decisions stalled.

One question separates the two engagements. Ask any candidate, including me: name the three decisions this engagement will force in the first 90 days.
