A stalled portco is not a failing company. That is what makes it dangerous.

Revenue holds. The team ships. The books close on time. No covenant breach, no payroll scare, no crisis. What stopped is the decisions.

Six signals define the pattern:

1. The headline metrics still look fine because they are computed against a customer definition that died quarters ago.

2. Board questions moved from "how fast can you grow" to "how long can you last."

3. The founder is managing the board instead of using it. Updates get longer and say less.

4. The raise that was supposed to start two quarters ago has not.

5. The finance function reports what happened with increasing polish and decreasing relevance.

6. Capital sits locked in dead-context decisions, and nobody owns naming it.

Three or more live at once and the company is in the category, whatever the dashboard says.

The clock starts around month 18 after the raise. That is when the plan written at the round stops answering questions, and a team that has not practiced deciding under a dead plan defaults to rolling everything forward.

The good news: stalled is recoverable, precisely because the underlying asset still works. Customers renew. The product functions. What broke is the decision layer, and decision layers can be rebuilt.

Full piece on the blog. Curious which signal others see first; for me it is almost always number 5.
