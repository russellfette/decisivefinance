## [SECTION 1: THE STUCK RAISE] · `#stuck-raise` · width: content (1180); body: prose (760)

### The Stuck Raise

Roughly seven thousand founders are trapped between rounds right now. Growth flatlined. Portfolio CFOs have pulled back. Growth-stage fractional CFOs will not touch a stalled cap table. The hardest forward calls of the company (product-line direction, pricing architecture, headcount shape, the path to the next round) are getting made on instinct, because the financial infrastructure to defend them to a board or a new lead is not there.

From the investor seat, the view is the same from the other side. Capital is deployed. Context has changed. The portfolio company is in what one board member called "narrative maintenance mode," reporting the same KPIs quarter after quarter. The unit economics are not clean enough to defend a follow-on, pass a reference check from a new lead, or walk into a board meeting with a story that holds up under pressure.

*The category belongs to the market. We are one operator inside it.*

## [SECTION 2: WHY NOT THE PORTFOLIO CFO] · width: content (1180); body: prose (760)

### Why Not the Portfolio CFO

**The portfolio CFO reports. Decisive Finance defends the next call.**

Different mandate, different output, no overlap. A portfolio CFO is paid to tell investors what happened last quarter. Decisive Finance is paid to decide what the next dollar does, defend the call with math, prove it in market, and install the rhythm that keeps doing it. The report can be clean and the next call still undefended. When the raise story needs defending, the founder does not need better reporting. They need every material next decision defended forward, executed, and proven in market.

Every month the discipline is absent, more capital stays deployed against a context that has already changed, and the raise story gets harder to defend.

## [SECTION 3: SIX TRAP DIAGNOSTIC] · `#traps` · width: content (1180); section background: `var(--ink)` dark

### Six structural patterns. Not founder failures.

Nobody runs the math on every commitment when growth is compounding. Not because they are lazy or unserious, because the human brain is built not to. The same six cognitive patterns quietly keep capital deployed against contexts that have changed:

**Sunk Cost.** Money already spent feels like it is voting. It is not.

**Status Quo.** Doing nothing feels neutral. It never is.

**Mental Accounting.** The right total hides the wrong allocation.

**Anchoring.** The first number set becomes the orbit.

**Loss Aversion.** The pain of admitting a gap is roughly twice the pleasure of recovering the capital.

**Certainty Illusion.** Polished reporting feels like evidence when it is actually theater.

Most stalled companies are running three or four at once. The traps are not failures of the founder. They are structural patterns no founder alone, however smart or diligent, can counteract while operating the business they created. We name the stack, surface it in the numbers, and convert each surfaced decision into a Three-Path forward call the board can follow. Financial Rhythms™ is the operating system that keeps the loop running after the engagement ends.

## [SECTION 5: WHAT RHYTHM INSTALLED MEANS] · width: content (1180); body: prose (760)

### What "Rhythm Installed" Means at Day 90

Tier 2 is not a second pull on the trapped-value well. Tier 1 already did that. Tier 2 is paid to install the rhythm that defends every next material call into in-market-tested growth, and compounds quarter over quarter. Three things have to be true on the Day 90 readout:

1. **Five material decisions defended forward with math.** Each with a Three-Path Model, a Six Trap stack naming the patterns that quietly held capital in place, the path forward, and a CEO-accepted recommendation on file.

2. **Two of those five live in market, with the data to score them.** Not modeled. Live. A pricing change with at least one cycle of data, a product-line restructure booked, a cost or headcount change implemented with documented impact, or a growth experiment running with named go and no-go triggers.

3. **A scenario forecast with a named path to profitability.** 18-month base, upside, downside. Weekly actuals-vs-prediction reconciliation running. Not a hope. A specific sequence of next decisions with named assumptions, triggers, and next-cycle hypotheses.

If all three are not true by Day 90, the rhythm is not installed. That is the test the guarantee measures against.

## [SECTION 6: ARTIFACT CATALOG] · `#artifacts` · width: content (1180); 3-column grid

### What Tier 2 produces. Ten new artifacts. Fifteen cumulative.

Not advisory hours. A specified product with specified output. Tier 1 delivers five artifacts. Tier 2 adds ten more on top. Fifteen cumulative through Day 90.

**1. Unit Economics Dashboard.** Live CAC, LTV, payback, gross and contribution margin, by segment, cohort, channel.

**2. Product Line P&Ls.** Contribution margin per product with full cost allocation and the cross-subsidy map nobody had.

**3. Forward Capital Register.** Every cost above $5,000 per month carries an explicit decision status, an owner, and a standing review cadence.

**4. Three-Path Models.** Keep / Kill / Restructure on every material decision, with the cognitive stack that quietly held the capital in place and the path forward.

**5. Scenario Forecast.** 18-month base, upside, downside, with named assumptions and weekly actuals-vs-prediction reconciliation.

**6. Pricing Architecture Revisit.** Repricing of existing offerings in existing channels. Not net-new products or channels.

**7. Growth Experiment Register.** The two live-in-market decisions with measurement protocol, go and no-go triggers.

**8. Bridge / Pivot / Raise Model.** Three financing paths including the capital plan the next round will be defended from.

**9. Board Decision Package.** Fully populated board deck the founder can walk through without hedging, plus a reusable template.

**10. Final Readout and Decision Playbook.** 90-day capstone and the forward playbook the CEO can execute without us.

### What's out of scope

If it is not in the artifact catalog, it is a change order. Explicitly outside Tier 2: ad-hoc modeling of unrelated transactions (acquisition offers, side investments), bookkeeping cleanup beyond what the artifacts require, board-meeting facilitation, HR or legal consulting on specific RIF cases, fundraising introductions or pitch coaching, interim controller or FP&A hiring, pricing architecture for net-new products or net-new channels, and growth experiments in channels the company is not already operating in. Any of those can be added as a change order with separate scope, separate price, and a fixed delivery date.

## [SECTION 8: PATTERN PROOF] · `#proof` · width: content (1180); 3-column case-card grid

### Pattern Proof · Three Mechanisms

The Decisive Finance mechanism works because the Six Trap Diagnostic™ reads the same way across funded tech contexts. These three engagements spanned SaaS, POS with hardware, and pre-seed AI. Stalled-tech Series A/B case studies are being built now, on a published timeline, through the Founding Cohort.

**Case card 1, Pathwright (SaaS / EdTech):**

*Outcome.* Valuation 3x. Net income 4x. LTV 2x to 5x.
*Trap stack.* Mental Accounting + Sunk Cost.
*Forward move.* $600,000 per year of engineering was customer-funded development hidden in the departmental P&L, deployed against a context that assumed it was all forward product investment. Product Line P&L broke the cross-subsidy. The path forward moved the work to paid services at 2.5x margin.
*What this means for stalled tech:* engineering-hidden-in-R&D is standard across funded SaaS, concentrated in 18-month-old hires whose original charter has since changed.

**Case card 2, SmartTab (Tech / POS):**

*Outcome.* 14 months cash stability. +19% valuation. Growth equity round secured.
*Trap stack.* Status Quo + Loss Aversion.
*Forward move.* Hardware ops running on a 2022 strategic decision nobody had re-decided against the current context. Founder anchored to the legacy model. The path forward made the emotion explicit and restructured to SaaS-led with partnered hardware. Zero cash crunches.
*What this means for stalled tech:* post-raise strategic commitments never re-decided against the current context are the largest single bucket of trapped value in the average funded tech P&L.

**Case card 3, Orchestraight (AI SaaS · Pre-seed):**

*Outcome.* $12M pre-seed cap closed. Board-ready from Day 1.
*Trap stack.* Mental Accounting + Status Quo + Anchoring + Certainty Illusion.
*Forward move.* Three revenue streams commingled. Pricing anchored to the wrong buyer. No scenario model for investors. Separated Enterprise / API / SMB economics forward, killed the money-losing $99 tier, sequenced API launch post-raise.
*What this means for stalled tech:* the investor-facing problem is identical. Commingled numbers, no downside case, no named assumption set. The raise is 18 months overdue, not next quarter.
